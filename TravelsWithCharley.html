AUG. 20-23 | Z. ZOLMER | S.R. LEE | CHAR. LEE

Travels with Charley in the P.N.W.

Replace J. Steinbeck with p.r. guy, French poodle for P.w.d.


What a shame to see this beautiful little blog, which for four months, three years ago, published deeply interesting articles on a topic of great importance, now just another indulgent travelogue, of a six-day roadtrip ft. a thirty-something couple and their dog.

What could be more unique? More urgent? Even the lowly Kings have more relevancy to you, the lowly reader.

Apparently I wanted to create this blog much more than I wanted to care for it. Meanwhile the Kings have been totally un-inspiring, which is normal and their prerogative, I just got tired of bridging the gap. And so this site, tho un-updated, stayed up, these last threee years.

So what better place than here, now? The title and overall theme still apply. In some way. I'll factor that in.

And if you're still reading this, you may care to know the list of books I'm bringing:

'Great American Bridges and Dams,' Donald C. Jackson, 1988

'One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest,' Ken Kesey, '62

'Road Trip USA...,' Jamie Jensen, 2002

'Sometimes a Great Notion,' Ken Kesey, '64

Plus a 2027 McNally road atlas ($20, B.&.N.), and a recent copy each of a New Yorker and Atlantic, my grandparents' subscrps., taken from a recent Wed. brunch in sunny suburban Lincoln, per routine. All of this rests inside a trademark Trader Joe's canvas bag, hung from the passenger's seat headrest, down the seat-back, for easy reaching.

A big part of bringing along this kind of crap is vanity, of course. But another factor is that, not only will we drive thru 'off-grid' or spotty-service areas, but I don't have an iphone. I have a flip-phone and an ipad (wi-fi only) now.

I'm told the flip-phone can in fact be a hot-spot, but I'm afraid that might break it to try, tho another one only costs $30. (The thought of more time in the customer-service chat-box is the real deterrent.)

So I'm leaning on ink and paper. I've got maps, guides, and literature, and at a fraction of the energy consumption of modern-day logistics (chips, phones, cell towers, servers, transm. lines, sub-stations, device re-charging, etc.).

(Oh yes, I forgot, I'm also bringing the thing I'm already reading, 'No Immediate Danger: Volume One of Carbon Ideologies' [2018], William T. Vollman.)

The careful reader may observe here that this technological limitation, totally self-imposed, meant to return value to the ink and paper, is less 'another' factor for bringing the books, than just a part of the 'vanity' part.

But wait. Do you want to be stirred, by language? Will you allow it? Try it? It doesn't mean it'll take. But it doesn't have to.

Here is the very start of 'Sometimes a Great Notion' then, which doesn't 'give anything away,' in case you're worried.

"Along the western slopes of the Oregon Coastal Range...come look: the hysterical crashing of tributaries as they merge into the Wakonda Auga River..."

And that's really very low-potency, as it should be, but gets the ball rolling. I winced first reading the "come look" narration, but it wasn't indicative of anything bad to come, and eventually my mind changed.

But here's the basic schedule, or itinerary, whichever is better.

But also, this lays everything out, and could even update, day by day, as we go, thru long stretches of forests, river valleys, and mountain towns, each probably guilty of nothing in reality, but myth-ologized thru 'Deliverance' (a fiction set in a wilderness two-thousand miles away) to great psychic effect. Point being, share this link among loved ones as you please, but I have enough (imagined) reason to worry, so don't get me or S.R. or the dog hunted and killed, inadvertently putting this beautiful, fragile little site before the red-rimmed eyes of some drug- or Facebook-crazed criminal-type.

DAY TWO -- Wed., Aug. 26 -- Manchester to Crescent City

S.R. 1, Mendocino, Fort Bragg, Hwy. 101, S.R. 254 ('Ave. of the Giants'), Hwy. 101, Eureka, Arcata, Elk Valley Casino parking lot OR Orman Ranch

(est. d.t.: 5:35)

Maybe entering new territory for me, when the 1 goes away from the coast after a ways. I think I saw it once, from a car with college friends, for a Portland trip, but I was trying to sleep most of the time.

But then, I haven't seen the 'Avenue of the Giants,' the sort of huge redwoods you can drive a car thru. Very much want to see this. The dappled light, the shade, the smell, come on.

"The stretch of US-101 through the redwood country of Humboldt County is lined by pristine groves of massive trees and provides boundless opportunities to come face-to-face with your own insignificance in nature's greater scheme of things. If you tire of this display of natural majesty, or simply want to keep it in context with the modern 'civilized' world, you're in luck: every few miles, amongst the stately trees, you'll come upon shameless souvenir stands selling redwood burl furniture and chainsaw sculptures..."

-- From p. 57 of 'Road Trip USA...' ('02)

Re: sleeping, the casino parking lot, per recent iOverlander user comment, may be restricted to more aesthetic, attractive-looking vans, like clean-kept Volkses or modern-day Sprinters. But if they dig the B.B., it's free, as long as I agree to obtain a free 'player's card' and write down my license and reg. numbers.

Whereas Orman is privately-owned camping, incl. for horses, at typical cost ($20-40). Got their number saved.


DAY THREE -- Thurs., Aug. 27 -- Cresc. City to Portland, Oreg.

Hwy. 101, Hwy. 199, Grants Pass, Ore. (birthpl. of the dog), I-5, Eugene (brief home of Z.), Hwy. 99, Corvallis, McMinville, Hwy. 99, I-5, airport (S.R. re-enters picture), Airbnb

(est. d.t.: 8:00)

Lots to explain here. I mean, not that you should care. Again, even the Kings, are probably more worth your time.

But we'll turn the camera on the dog when we re-enter Grants Pass (hold the S.C.o.t.U.S. jokes), to see if she makes any visible sign of knowing. In my experience, with dogs, not likely. But I always look. And I have always loved them, and love her, just the same. Not that you asked.

But, make no mistake, Char. Lee was no 'rescue.' She was product of a P.w.d. 'puppy mill.' S.R. and I walked into this house, which smelled appropriately of dog, met Char. Lee (then 'Miss Candy'), who bit our hands and wrestled her dad, the big black 'Bear,' who was at some point secreted out of the room, clearing the way for me and S.R. to take his offspring, which we did, after a $2,500 Zelle transfer, not to Bear of course.

This is the original sin. We cannot possibly compensate as owners or pet-parents or whatever for the crime and pain of taking the dog from her real parents. Does she love us? Can she? Is that love? Or stockholm symdrome?

But then, we find Eugene, a place I've barely seen since sadly attending college there for two years, ten years ago, but that I tend to remember fondly for some reason, maybe as long as the memory doesn't take place in a dorm, apartment, or classroom.

But I want to see my dorm building again, which was (is?) off-campus, called a name beginning with a 'T,' I think. Want to see the campus. Maybe the dog and I walk the route I used to go. Want to see the ol' apartment building. The river. Then all that's new.

Then onto Corvallis and McMinville for more memory masturbation, in the respective homes of Oregon State and Linfield Univ., campuses that J. and S. Palmer and I toured one summer ('13), on a three-day trip.

Then, onto the Portland airport, to retrieve my... baggage... Just kidding! I mean my love! My girlfriend! S.R. Lee! She enjoys and responds to that sort of humor, trust me. Her flight lands at 10:00pm.

Southwest had a nice little deal, and S.R. booked the in-laws-style rental. I understand the airport's undergone extensive recent renovations. I liked it fine already, personally. But what is this money going to airports? It's like they cannot stop renovating. New parking garage here, new walkway there. I'm not complaining except, it's suspicious.

FURTHER READING

Background, context, and other addl. information.

The Vehicle

Lovingly dubbed 'The Brown Bomber' by J. Palmer, the 2008 Chrysler Town & Country LX, which takes ethanol, is now, this month, middle-age: one-hundred-and-fifty-thousand miles.

I think I've done eighty-thousand of those. I paid my literate liberal Lincoln-based grandparents $10 for the car, in 2017. They'd bought a nice white Kia something, first in order to go on their own, more impressive roadtrip, East Coast and back, much of it recorded via dashboard GoPro. I used the van for Davis commuting.

I looked up (asked an A.I. product about) the dollar-value of the van, if sold today, to like a CarMax, and it estimated: $1,500.

This poor van, which nobody believes in, supposing it'll break down and strand us at any moment, not that it ever has, but it looks like it has, is our shelter. These poor people never learned to judge a car by the content of its character, apparently.

Plus, this trip has inspired me to finally Windex the windows, wipe down the walls, and most notably, update the van's registration, after a four-year break.

Juuust before that update occurred, however, an I-80 C.H.P. officer, S. Birdseye, pulled me over just before the Scandia beside the freeway, at the Madison overpass.

In a pathetic desperation, I held up to him my ipad, when he came to the passenger's window, showing an e-mail that outlined my $812 debt to the D.M.V.

"I'm about to pay it!!" I assured him.

"Why haven't you paid it in the last four years?" Birdseye asked.

"No reason. Laziness."

"Uh, okay. You know, six months past expiration, your car can be impounded."

"I've been told that."

"What. When?"

"I got pulled over in Stateline three years ago, right when I crossed over."

"Oh, they were waitin' for ya, huh?"

(Note to international reader: In Calif., and maybe others, you must re-confirm the 'registration' of your car constantly, exchanging paperwork in a process that employs people, and sustains the authorities. Then, they may disrupt your day, for as little reason as having late paperwork [indicated by color of license-plate sticker], taking hundreds of your dollars, or even your whole car, if they feel like it, after six mos., I've been told.)

Truth be told, this trip was the 'carrot' for finally squaring the van's registration. Having the thing impounded, hundreds of miles from home, would pretty well ruin everything, I imagined. (My parents consider this a reaction to the 'stick,' still.)

Its Contents

From the front seat, looking back over your shoulder, the bed runs down the right side (pillow at far-end), then a slim aisle along the left side, with clothes, toiletries, towels, a sleeping bag, a backup tent, skateboards, a gas can, and a Coleman grill tucked under the raised, Home Depot-plywood bedframe.

The van's two under-floor compartments, opened via ring latch, hold motor oil, jumper cables, an extension cord, and a toolset, and trash bags, plastic bags, duct tape, straps, twine, clothes-pins, tennis balls, and a frisbee.

On the bed there's a 'kitchen' bin, a dog bin, a black cylindrical 'bear' bin (food), and a shoe-box (food). In the aisle, on their sides, are two adjustable, folded-up Lifetime tables. The blue seven-gallon water cube, with spout, sits on the floor behind the passenger's seat.

The Weather

A month or so ago, R. Klaeser stepped out of his car, in Sac., breathed in the hot air, and said, "God, I've missed this weather."

Well, that may be so. But if you're living in this valley, regularly, there's nothing to miss. It is just hot, day after day. And it isn't even that bad. But it's bad enough.

Conversely, the weather along the coast, from Bodega Bay to Cresc. City, is right now 50s/60s and semi-sunny. Grants Pass becomes warmer, maybe touching 90, while Eugene is cooler slightly; same for Corvallis and McMinville, and Portland should be just under 80. The coast from Tillamook down forecasts like Calif.'s, maybe with more chance of rain.

The Food

Last week I killed some time (I'm un-employed) wandering thru IKEA, for a $15 French press ('UPPHETTA'). So that, filled with T.J.'s medium-roast pre-ground, will start our days. Bottom-shelf red wines will end them.

I've got white gluten-free T.J.'s sandwich bread, almond butter, and jam (reduced-sugar raspberry) for mid-day sandwiches. I've got rice and cans of beans (one black, one pinto) for dinners. I've got little squares of salt, ground pepper, garlic powder, basil, and red-pepper flakes in a days-of-the-week pill-box.

But once S.R. re-enters the picture, with her tastes and talents, dinners could improve. We'll shop each day for ingredients. So vegetables or fruits could play parts. Or meats, cheeses, etc.

And we'll eat using, what else, speckled enamelware, red and blue little bowls, with matching light-weight mugs. Our utensils are chopsticks and sporks.

Meanwhile the dog gets brown kibble and canned T.J.'s pink salmon, with 'burger jerky' (bison) and marrow bones (also bison) as treats. And water.
